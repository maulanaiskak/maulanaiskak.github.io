Overview

Control Valve is the final control element of a control loop. Control Valve will then implement the strategy chosen by the controller. The control valve manipulates the fluid flowing in the pipe, such as gas, steam, water, or chemical compounds to keep the process variable at its setpoint value.

Stiction comes from the words static and friction which means static friction. Stiction is a problem with the control valve that occurs due to the increase in the value of static friction on the control valve stem so that a stronger force is needed so that the valve can move. The value of static friction on the control valve stem can increase due to seal degradation, lubricant depletion, foreign matter ingress, activation of the metal sliding surface causing high temperatures, and too tight packing around the stem. Resistance caused by packing on the stem is often considered to be the main cause of stiction. Another very common cause of stiction is due to regulations regarding the emission of volatile organic compounds (VOC). When a VOC leak occurs, the packing will be tightened considerably more than necessary, causing valve jams and making the process less efficient due to increased energy consumption. When stiction occurs, the control valve stem does not respond to the output signal from the controller so that the movement of the control valve is disturbed and causes the relationship between the controller output and valve position to be non-linear. Stiction can cause plant-wide oscillations and increase process variability.
