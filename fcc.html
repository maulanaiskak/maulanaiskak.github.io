Fluid Catalytic Cracking

instrumentation and control system

Overview

Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC) is one of the processing units in oil refineries. The main input for the FCC is gas oil, which is a heavy, long-chain, high-boiling hydrocarbon molecule. FCC breaks the carbon chain in the molecule so that it becomes shorter and has lighter carbon bonds. FCC consists of a feed system, preheating system, reactor, regenerator, compressor, air blower, catalyst circulation, and fractionation tower. Preheated feed is mixed with hot slurry recycled from the bottom of the main fractionator and injected at the bottom of the riser along with the hot catalyst from the regenerator. This totally vaporizes the feed. The hot catalyst provides the sensible heat, the heat of vaporization, and the heat of reaction necessary for the endothermic cracking reaction. In the riser, coke is deposited on the catalyst and hence the catalyst has to be regenerated in the regenerator by burning off the coke in the presence of air. The air is provided to the regenerator by two centrifugal air blowers: the combustion air blower (which provides the bulk air) and the lift air blower (which also assists in altering the density of the catalyst for circulation). The reactor product gas is passed to the main fractionator for heat recovery and separation into various products.

The spent catalyst is transported back to the regenerator through a U-bend tube. Air is injected at the bottom of the regenerator lift pipe to assist in the circulation of the catalyst. Catalyst in the regenerator is fluidized using the air from the lift and combustion air blowers. Carbon and hydrogen on the catalyst react with the air to form carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and water. The flue gas travels up the regenerator through the cyclones, where the entrained catalyst is removed. Regenerated catalyst flows over a weir into the regenerator standpipe. The level of the catalyst in the stand-pipe provides the driving force for the catalyst flow back to the reactor.
