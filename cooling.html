Wet Cooling Water

temperature and level control system

Overview

Cooling towers are heat exchangers that are used to dissipate large heat loads to the atmosphere. They are used as an important component in many industrial and commercial processes needing to dissipate heat. Wet cooling towers rely on the latent heat of water evaporation to exchange heat between the process and the air passing through the cooling tower. The cooling water may be an integral part of the process or may provide cooling via heat exchangers.

In the process of a steam power plant, the steam that has undergone expansion in the turbine will be condensed in the condenser and then pumped into the boiler to form a closed system (water and steam cycle). One of the mechanisms for condensing steam in the condenser is to use direct contact, meaning that the steam will be directly contacted with cooling water so that dew (saturated liquid) is formed in the condenser pool. The condensate that forms is often referred to as condensate. Part of the condensate water will be pumped into the boiler and some will be cooled in the cooling tower and then reused as cooling water.
